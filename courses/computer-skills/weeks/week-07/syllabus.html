Learning Objectives

  • Explain what artificial intelligence is in simple, practical terms
  • Access ChatGPT and create a free account
  • Navigate the ChatGPT interface confidently
  • Write effective prompts that get helpful responses
  • Apply AI tools to practical tasks: writing, planning, and learning
  • Understand AI limitations and practice safe usage
  • Complete a multi-part hands-on workshop using AI independently
  • Test AI accuracy by fact-checking responses against known information

Instructor Notes

WIPPEA Pacing & Workshop Logistics

  • Distribute the AI Tools Workshop handout at the 0:35 mark when guided practice begins.
  • Parts 1-2 are instructor-led (guided). Parts 3-4 are student-driven (independent).
  • Pacing: Account creation can eat into time. If needed, shorten the presentation and go straight to guided practice.

Key Teaching Points

  • Address fears early: Some students may have concerns about AI "taking over" or being dangerous. Acknowledge these concerns and focus on AI as a helpful tool, like a calculator or search engine.
  • Start simple: Begin with basic prompts before moving to complex ones. "What is the capital of France?" before "Help me write a letter."
  • Emphasize the conversation: AI works best when you have a back-and-forth. If the first answer isn't right, ask for changes!
  • Watch for frustration: Account creation can be tricky. Have students help each other and be ready to assist.
  • Real-world relevance: Use veteran-specific examples (VA correspondence, benefits questions, family communication).
  • Safety is paramount: Reinforce NEVER sharing personal information. This is crucial for this population.

Common Questions & Answers

Be Prepared For:

  • "Is this spying on me?" - ChatGPT doesn't know who you are unless you tell it. Don't share personal info and you're safe.
  • "Does it cost money?" - The basic version is free. Plus version ($20/mo) exists but isn't necessary.
  • "Can it write my emails for me?" - Yes! That's one of its best uses. Always review before sending.
  • "Will it replace jobs?" - AI is a tool that helps humans work, like a hammer helps a carpenter. It doesn't replace the person.
  • "How does it know so much?" - It learned from reading billions of web pages, like someone who read every book in every library.

Discussion Questions

Warm-Up

  1. What do you think AI can do? What can't it do? Any fears or excitement?

After Presentation

  1. How is asking AI a question different from doing a Google search?

Evaluation

  1. What was your best prompt? Did you find anything ChatGPT got wrong?

Wrap-Up

  1. What's one AI task you'll try at home this week? Why is it important to always verify?

Assessment Checklist

By End of Class, Students Should Be Able To:

  • ☐ Log into ChatGPT independently
  • ☐ Type a simple question and receive a response
  • ☐ Explain the difference between a weak and strong prompt
  • ☐ Write a prompt asking for help with a specific task
  • ☐ List at least 3 things they should NEVER share with AI
  • ☐ Describe one practical use for AI in their own life
