Week 3: Email Productivity (Gmail)

VUB Intermediate Computer Skills

Instructor Guide
📅 Week 3 of 8 ⏱️ 2 Hours (WIPPEA) 👥 Veterans Group 📍 Computer Lab

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

  • Navigate the Gmail interface confidently
  • Create and color-code labels to organize emails
  • Compose professional emails with proper etiquette
  • Understand CC, BCC, and Reply All usage
  • Add attachments and manage file sizes
  • Create a professional email signature
  • Identify phishing emails and use search operators
  • Apply skills independently through guided workshop
⏰ WIPPEA Session Timeline (120 Minutes)
0:00-0:08

Inbox Challenge Warm-Up

Have students open Gmail and check their inbox count. Round-robin share: "How many unread emails? Can you find a specific email from last week?" Surface common frustrations (can't find emails, too much junk, unsure if safe). Connect to Week 2 safety skills.

0:08-0:15

Today's Roadmap Introduction

Present the 2-hour agenda using the Roadmap slide. Preview what they'll build: organized inbox with labels, professional signature, 3 scenario emails. Distribute the Gmail Mastery Workshop handout now so they can see the full plan.

0:15-0:35

Core Presentation Presentation

Move briskly through slides 3-15. Key topics: Gmail interface layout, labels vs folders concept, creating and color-coding labels, search operators, email composition and etiquette, CC/BCC/Reply All, attachments, contacts, signatures, spam and phishing review. Show on projector; keep explanations concise since students will practice each skill hands-on in the workshop.

0:35-0:55

Workshop Parts 1 & 2 Guided Practice

Part 1 - Inbox Organization Sprint (15 min): Walk through creating 5 color-coded labels step-by-step on projector. Students follow along using their workshop handout. Help them apply labels to existing emails, star important ones, archive old messages.

Part 2 - Signature Studio (10 min): Have students draft their signature on paper first (name, branch, phone, email). Then walk through Gmail Settings together to create it. They send a test email to themselves to verify.

0:55-1:05

Break Break

Remind students to save their work before stepping away. Check that labels and signatures are saved. Students stretch, use restroom, refill coffee. Quick check-in with any students who are behind.

1:05-1:45

Workshop Parts 3 & 4 Independent Practice

Part 3 - Email Composition Challenge (20 min): Students work independently on 3 scenario emails (VA appointment, classmate recommendation, vendor complaint). Each has a rubric checklist on the handout. Circulate to provide feedback.

Part 4 - Security & Search Power-ups (15 min): Students identify red flags on phishing examples, practice search operators to find old emails, and optionally set up a filter. Early finishers help neighbors or try bonus challenges.

1:45-1:55

Show & Share Evaluation

Partner check: students pair up and show their labeled inbox, signature, and one scenario email. Partners give feedback using the checklist criteria. Ask for volunteers to share one useful thing they learned. Use Knowledge Check questions (slide 18) for quick formative assessment.

1:55-2:00

Email Challenge & Preview Application

Assign the at-home challenge: organize 10 real emails, write a VA records request, use a search operator, check Spam folder. Remind them to keep their workshop handout as a reference. Preview Week 4: Microsoft Word Intermediate.

📝 Instructor Notes

  • Gmail Accounts: Verify everyone has a Gmail account at the start. Some may need help logging in or may need to create one. Budget extra time at warm-up if needed.
  • Pacing the Presentation: The lecture portion is only 20 minutes. Move briskly through concepts - students will practice each skill hands-on during the workshop. Don't linger on slides they'll repeat in practice.
  • Workshop Handout: Distribute the Gmail Mastery Workshop handout during the Introduction phase. Students should have it in front of them before guided practice begins.
  • Guided vs. Independent: Parts 1 & 2 are instructor-led (show step, wait, confirm). Parts 3 & 4 are student-driven (circulate, answer questions, don't demo every step).
  • Labels Concept: The "one email, multiple labels" concept can be confusing. Use the "colored stickers on a document" analogy from the presentation.
  • Etiquette Sensitivity: Some may feel their current email habits are being criticized. Frame it as "professional contexts" not "right vs wrong."
  • Signature Privacy: Remind them not to include sensitive info (SSN, full address) in signatures - just name, phone, email.
  • Early Finishers: Have them help neighbors (peer teaching reinforces learning) or try the bonus challenges in Part 4 of the workshop.

💬 Discussion Prompts by Phase

  • Warm-Up (Inbox Challenge): "How many unread emails do you have? Can you find an email from last week in under 30 seconds? What's the most frustrating thing about your inbox?"
  • Presentation (Labels): "What categories make sense for YOUR life? VA documents, family, bills, hobbies? Think of 5 labels you'd actually use."
  • Presentation (Etiquette): "Has anyone received an email that rubbed you the wrong way? What about it bothered you? (Don't share names!)"
  • Evaluation (Show & Share): "What was the most useful thing you learned today? Any tips or tricks you discovered on your own during the workshop?"
  • Application (Wrap-Up): "What's one thing you'll do differently with email this week? Who will you send your first properly-formatted email to?"

⚠️ Common Issues to Watch For

Gmail Account Access: Some may have forgotten passwords or have old accounts. Budget 5-10 extra minutes at start if needed.

Two-Factor Authentication: If their Gmail has 2FA, they'll need their phone to log in. Make sure they have it.

Different Gmail Versions: Some may have "Classic" Gmail view. The new view is default - help them switch if needed.

Attachment Location: Participants often struggle to find files on their computer. Show them how to navigate to Documents/Downloads.

Workshop Pacing: Some students will finish Parts 3 & 4 early. Direct them to the bonus challenges or have them assist neighbors. Others may not finish all 3 scenario emails - that's OK, they can complete at home.

✅ Tips for Success

  • Walk around constantly during workshop activities - don't stay at the front
  • During guided practice, show each step slowly, wait for everyone, then proceed
  • During independent practice, let students work at their own pace and ask for help
  • Have early finishers help neighbors (peer teaching reinforces learning)
  • If someone gets lost during guided practice, help them catch up before moving on
  • The workshop handout has checkboxes - encourage students to check off each step
  • Connect back to Week 2 - suspicious emails, phishing awareness in inbox
  • Emphasize the at-home challenge: real practice cements the skills
