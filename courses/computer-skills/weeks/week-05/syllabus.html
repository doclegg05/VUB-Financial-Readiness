🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

  • Understand cells, rows, columns, and cell addresses
  • Create basic formulas: SUM, AVERAGE, MAX, MIN, COUNT
  • Sort data in ascending or descending order
  • Filter data to show only specific items
  • Create a simple bar or pie chart from data
  • Build a basic personal budget spreadsheet
  • Apply conditional formatting to highlight over/under budget spending
  • Complete a multi-part hands-on workshop independently
⏰ WIPPEA Session Timeline (120 Minutes)
0:00-0:08

Money Mystery Warm-Up

Quick mental math challenges: "You spend $12.50 on lunch for 5 days — total?" Motivates learning formulas. Discussion: What numbers do you deal with regularly? Bills? Prescriptions? Mileage?

0:08-0:15

Why Excel & Today's Roadmap Introduction

Preview learning objectives: formulas, sorting, filtering, charts. Show the finished budget tracker they'll build. Connect to veteran life: tracking VA copays, monthly bills, medication costs.

Excel Essentials Presentation

Condensed lecture covering: cells/rows/columns, formula basics (start with =), SUM, AVERAGE, MAX, MIN, COUNT, sorting A-Z/Z-A, filtering with dropdown arrows, chart types (bar, pie, line). Use slides with interactive excel-grid examples.

Workshop Parts 1 & 2 Guided Practice

Distribute Excel Budget Workshop handout. Part 1 (10 min): Create budget with 8 categories, format as currency. Part 2 (15 min, starts at ~0:45): SUM, AVERAGE, MAX, MIN formulas, fill handle, difference column. Walk around constantly — cell selection trips people up.

This week: track real spending daily in your budget. Update the Actual column each evening. Bring your completed budget to Week 6 — we'll save it to Google Drive so you can access it from any device! Preview: Cloud Computing & Google Drive.

📊 Key Formulas Reference

  • =SUM(A1:A10) — Adds up all values from A1 to A10
  • =AVERAGE(A1:A10) — Calculates the average (mean)
  • =MAX(A1:A10) — Finds the highest value
  • =MIN(A1:A10) — Finds the lowest value
  • =COUNT(A1:A10) — Counts how many cells contain numbers

📝 Instructor Notes

  • Equals Sign: The #1 mistake is forgetting to start formulas with =. Emphasize this repeatedly.
  • Cell References: Explain why we use A1 instead of typing "10" - if data changes, the formula updates automatically.
  • Range Notation: The colon means "through" - A1:A10 means "A1 through A10".
  • AutoSum Shortcut: Alt+= is a huge time-saver. Demo it multiple times.
  • Patience with Clicking: Many will struggle to select the right cells. Walk around and assist.
  • Real-World Connection: Use veteran-relevant examples: tracking VA appointment costs, medication expenses, or gas mileage.
  • Workshop Handout: Distribute the Excel Budget Workshop handout at the 0:35 mark when guided practice begins.
  • Guided vs. Independent: Parts 1-2 are instructor-led (guided). Walk through each step together. Parts 3-4 are student-driven (independent) — circulate and assist individually.
  • Pacing: If the class is ahead, extend the independent practice. If behind, skip Part 4 bonus challenges and go straight to evaluation.

💬 Discussion Questions

  • Warm-Up: "What do you spend money on every month? Groceries, bills, gas? What if you could track all of it in one place?"
  • After Presentation: "Can you think of things in your life where you add up numbers? Monthly bills? Groceries? That's exactly what SUM is for!"
  • Guided Practice: "When might you want to sort data from highest to lowest? What about alphabetically?"
  • Evaluation: "Which formula will you use most at home? Was anything about formulas surprising?"
  • Wrap-up: "What's one real expense you'll start tracking in Excel this week?"

✅ Tips for Success

  • Walk around constantly during formula practice - cell selection trips people up
  • Show the same formula multiple times in different contexts
  • If someone's formula shows an error, check: Did they start with =? Is the range correct?
  • Encourage peer help - those who "get it" can assist neighbors
  • Keep the budget activity simple - 4-5 categories is plenty
  • Celebrate small wins - "Your formula worked! Excel just did math for you!"
