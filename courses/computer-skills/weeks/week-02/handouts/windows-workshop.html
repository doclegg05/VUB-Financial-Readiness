Part 1: Keyboard Shortcuts Boot Camp

~15 minGUIDED

Step 1 — Open Notepad

  • Open Notepad using a keyboard shortcut Press the Windows key on your keyboard (between Ctrl and Alt). Type "notepad" (just start typing!). Press Enter — Notepad opens!

Step 2 — Type a Practice Paragraph

  • Type: “I am learning keyboard shortcuts at VUB today.”

Step 3 — Try Copy and Paste

  • Copy and paste using keyboard shortcuts Press Ctrl + A to select ALL text. Press Ctrl + C to COPY. Press Enter twice to make blank lines. Press Ctrl + V to PASTE.

Step 4 — Try Undo and Redo

  • Undo and redo your changes Press Ctrl + Z to UNDO (paste disappears!). Press Ctrl + Z again (blank lines disappear!). Press Ctrl + Y to REDO (brings it back!).

Step 5 — Try Cut

  • Cut and paste text Select all text with Ctrl + A. Press Ctrl + X to CUT (text disappears — it is on your clipboard!). Press Ctrl + V to paste it back.

Step 6 — Save Your File

  • Save with a keyboard shortcut Press Ctrl + S to SAVE. Save to Desktop as “Shortcuts Practice”.
Tip: Think of C-V-X as: C = Copy, V = paste down (like a V arrow), X = cut (like scissors X).

Part 2: Window Management & Multitasking

~15 minGUIDED

Step 1 — Open Three Programs

  • Open 3 programs at once Notepad (already open). File Explorer: press Windows + E. Your web browser: click its icon on the taskbar.

Step 2 — Snap Windows Side by Side

  • Snap two windows side by side Click on Notepad to make it active. Press Windows + ← — it snaps to the left half! Click on File Explorer. Press Windows + → — it snaps to the right half!

Step 3 — Switch Between Windows

  • Switch windows with Alt+Tab Press Alt + Tab — a preview shows all open windows. Keep holding Alt and press Tab again to cycle through. Release to switch to that window.

Step 4 — Maximize and Minimize

  • Maximize and minimize with keyboard Press Windows + ↑ to MAXIMIZE a window. Press Windows + ↓ to RESTORE it (or minimize if already restored).

Step 5 — Pin a Program to the Taskbar

  • Pin a program to your taskbar Right-click the Notepad icon on the taskbar. Click “Pin to taskbar.” Now it stays there even after you close Notepad!
Tip: Snapping windows is great for following instructions on one side while working on the other side — like following this workshop!

Part 3: Screenshots & Snipping Tool

~15 minINDEPENDENT

Step 1 — Full Screen Screenshot

  • Take a full-screen screenshot Press Print Screen key (might say PrtScn on your keyboard). Open Paint (Windows key, type “paint”). Press Ctrl + V to paste your screenshot there.

Step 2 — Active Window Screenshot

  • Capture just one window Click on a window to make it active. Press Alt + Print Screen (captures only that window!). Open Paint and press Ctrl + V.

Step 3 — Snip & Sketch (Best Method!)

  • Use Snip & Sketch to capture a selection Press Windows + Shift + S. Your screen dims — draw a rectangle around what you want. A notification appears — click it to open Snip & Sketch. Click the save icon to save as an image.

Step 4 — Save Your Screenshot

  • Save your screenshot as a file In Snip & Sketch or Paint, press Ctrl + S. Save to your Desktop as “My Screenshot.”
Tip: Windows+Shift+S is the MOST USEFUL screenshot shortcut. It works everywhere and lets you select exactly what you want.
Caution: Screenshots can capture sensitive information (passwords, personal data). Be careful what is on screen before sharing screenshots.

Part 4: Personalize & Accessibility

~15 minINDEPENDENT

Step 1 — Make Text Larger

  • Increase display scale Right-click on the Desktop → click “Display settings.” Find “Scale and layout” section. Change “Scale” from 100% to 125%. Everything gets bigger and easier to read!

Step 2 — Try the Magnifier

  • Use the Magnifier tool Press Windows + + to turn on Magnifier. Move your mouse around — the screen zooms in! Press Windows + + again to zoom in more. Press Windows + - to zoom out. Press Windows + Esc to turn off Magnifier.

Step 3 — Change Your Desktop Wallpaper

  • Personalize your desktop background Right-click on the Desktop → “Personalize.” Click “Background.” Choose a picture or solid color you like.

Step 4 — Adjust Mouse Pointer Size (Optional)

  • Make the mouse pointer larger Open Settings (Windows + I). Go to “Accessibility” → “Mouse pointer and touch.” Drag the slider to make the pointer larger. Try different pointer styles.

Step 5 — Try Night Light (Optional)

  • Turn on Night Light for easier viewing Open Settings → System → Display. Find “Night light” and turn it on. Makes the screen warmer/less blue — easier on eyes at night.
Tip: You can always change these settings back! Nothing here is permanent. Experiment and find what is comfortable for you.
