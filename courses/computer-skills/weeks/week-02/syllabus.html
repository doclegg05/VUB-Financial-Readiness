Key Teaching Points

  • Start with the Speed Challenge — it immediately shows the value of shortcuts and hooks engagement
  • During the presentation, DEMONSTRATE each shortcut live on the projector — don't just show slides
  • Let students try each shortcut immediately after you demonstrate it (2-3 attempts)
  • During Workshop Parts 1-2 (guided), go SLOWLY — these students are learning muscle memory
  • During Workshop Parts 3-4 (independent), circulate and help — screenshots and settings can be confusing
  • Some students may already know Ctrl+C/V — encourage them to help neighbors and learn newer shortcuts (Win+Shift+S, Win+Arrow)
  • Emphasize that ALL shortcuts work in every program they'll learn (Word, Excel, Gmail)

WIPPEA Pacing & Workshop Logistics

  • Distribute Windows Workshop handout at 0:35 (start of Practice phase)
  • Distribute Keyboard Shortcuts Reference Card at 1:55 (Application phase — they take it home)
  • Parts 1-2 are GUIDED — instructor demonstrates each step, students follow along
  • Parts 3-4 are INDEPENDENT — students work at own pace with instructor support
  • The Show & Share is important — it builds confidence when students demo to each other
  • Connect to next week: "Your Copy/Paste and Save shortcuts will be essential for Gmail next week!"

Discussion Questions (by WIPPEA Phase)

  • Warm-up: "How do you usually open programs — Start menu, desktop icons, or taskbar?"
  • After Shortcuts: "Which shortcut do you think you'll use the most? Which surprised you?"
  • After Windows: "When would snapping two windows side-by-side be useful in real life?"
  • After Screenshots: "What would you want to take a screenshot of? Error messages? Recipes? Directions?"
  • Evaluation: "What was the hardest shortcut to remember? What trick helps you remember it?"
