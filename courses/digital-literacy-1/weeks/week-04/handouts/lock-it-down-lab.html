Station 1: Build a Strong Passphrase

~4 minGUIDED

Step 1 — Pick Three Unrelated Words

  • Choose three random, unrelated words Example: trout, coffee, sunrise. Avoid names, birthdays, or anything someone could guess about you.

Step 2 — Join Them & Add a Twist

  • Connect the words and mix in a capital letter and a number Example: PurpleTrout-Coffee-Sunrise7. Aim for at least 12 characters — longer is stronger.

Tip: Length beats complexity. A long passphrase of plain words is both harder to crack and easier to remember than a short jumble like "P@ss1!".
Caution: Use a different passphrase for each important account, and never write it on a sticky note on your monitor. Consider a password manager to remember them for you.

Station 2: Lock Your Screen

~2 minGUIDED

Step 1 — Lock With a Shortcut

  • Press the lock-screen shortcut Press Windows + L. Your screen locks right away. Sign back in with your password or PIN.

Step 2 — Set Automatic Locking (Optional)

  • Have Windows lock by itself when you step away Open Settings (Windows + I) → Accounts → Sign-in options. Under "If you've been away, when should Windows require you to sign in again?" choose a short time.
Tip: Get in the habit of pressing Windows + L any time you leave your seat — at the lab, the library, or a clinic waiting room.

Checkpoint — check the box when done:

  • ☐ I locked my screen with Windows + L and signed back in

Station 3: Clear Your Browser Data

~3 minGUIDED

Step 1 — Open "Clear Browsing Data"

  • Open the clear-data window In Chrome or Edge, press Ctrl + Shift + Delete. (Or click the three dots ⋮ in the top-right → Settings → Privacy.)

Step 2 — Choose What to Clear

  • Select history, cookies, and cached files Pick a time range, such as "Last hour" or "All time." Then click Clear data.
Heads up: Clearing cookies signs you out of websites — that's expected. You'll just sign back in next time.
Tip: Always clear your data and sign out of your accounts before leaving a shared or public computer.

Checkpoint — check the box when done:

  • ☐ I cleared my browsing history and cookies

Station 4: Open a Private Window

~3 minGUIDED

Step 1 — Open Private / Incognito Mode

  • Open a private window In Chrome, press Ctrl + Shift + N (Incognito). In Edge or Firefox, press Ctrl + Shift + P (InPrivate). Notice the dark window and the icon.

Step 2 — Visit a Site, Then Close It

  • Browse, then close the private window Visit any website, then close the window. That browsing won't be saved in your history on this device.
Important: Private mode does NOT make you invisible. Your internet provider, the network you're on, and any site you log into can still see you. It only avoids saving history and cookies on this computer.

Station 5: Is This Source Trustworthy?

~4 minINDEPENDENT

Step 1 — Pick a Web Page to Check

  • Choose any article or page your instructor suggests (or search a health or news topic) You'll judge whether you can trust what it says.

Step 2 — Run the Validity Checklist

Ask...Your answer (Yes / No / Not sure)
Is there a named author or organization? 
Is the site trustworthy (.gov, major news, known org)? 
Is the information current (has a recent date)? 
Is the tone calm and factual (not shocking/urgent)? 
Can I confirm it on a second trusted source? 
Tip: Mostly "Yes" answers? Likely trustworthy. Several "No" answers, or it asks for money or personal info? Be skeptical and verify elsewhere before believing or sharing it.

  • ☐ I evaluated a source and decided whether I trust it

Station 7: What's Stored on This PC?

~3 minINDEPENDENT

Step 1 — Look in Your Downloads Folder

  • Open File Explorer and check Downloads Press Windows + E, then click "Downloads" on the left. These files live on this computer — anyone using it could open them.

Step 2 — Check for Saved Passwords

  • See if your browser is saving passwords In Chrome or Edge, open the three-dot menu → Settings → Passwords. On a shared computer, you do NOT want passwords saved here.
Why it matters: Downloaded files, saved passwords, and autofill details all stay on the device. If it's lost, stolen, or shared, that information could be exposed. Remove what you don't need and don't save passwords on shared PCs.

Station 8: Desk Ergonomics Check

~3 minINDEPENDENT

Adjust Your Workstation

  • Screen distanceSit so the screen is about an arm's length away.
  • Screen heightThe top of the screen should be at or just below eye level, so you look slightly down.
  • Chair & backSit back in the chair with your lower back supported; feet flat on the floor.
  • Wrists & armsKeep wrists straight and elbows near 90 degrees while typing.
  • Eye breaks (20-20-20)Every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet away for 20 seconds.
Tip: Stand up and stretch every half hour. Small adjustments now prevent neck, back, wrist, and eye strain later.
