⏰ Session Timeline (2 Hours)
0:00-0:10

Welcome & Warm-Up Warm-Up

Greet the group. Ask: "Have you ever gotten a suspicious email or text pretending to be from the VA, a bank, or a delivery company?" Let a few share. Distribute the two handouts.

0:10-0:35

Digital Identity, PII & Reputation Lecture

Slides 2-5. Cover digital identity and managing personal data (2.1), what PII is and how to protect it, permanence of online actions, and legal/ethical behavior (2.2). Use the "grandkid test" framing.

0:35-0:55

Responding to Inappropriate Content Discussion

Slides 6-8. Impact of negative communication (2.3.1), checking validity of online info (2.3.2), anonymity (2.3.3), and the value of nonresponse — block, report, walk away (2.3.4). Invite real examples.

0:55-1:05

Break Break

10-minute break. Encourage participants to stand, stretch, and rest their eyes — a natural tie-in to today's ergonomics topic.

1:05-1:30

Security, Passwords & Tracking Presentation

Slides 9-15. Security threats (7.1); strong passphrases, reset, lock, clear browser data (7.2); tracking and stored-data risks (7.3.3), private browsing and its limits (7.3.4); mental and physical/ergonomic health (7.4).

1:30-1:55

"Lock It Down" Hands-On Lab Practice

Participants work through the lab on their own PCs: build a passphrase, lock the screen (Windows + L), clear browser history/cookies, open a private window, evaluate a source, run the privacy self-audit, check what's stored on the PC, and do a desk ergonomics check. Walk the room and assist.

1:55-2:00

Knowledge Check & Wrap-Up Application

Run the 3-question knowledge check on the last slides. Recap key takeaways. Preview Week 5: Review & Testing Day (cumulative recap + Post-Test). Encourage students to keep the quick-reference card at their desk.

📝 Instructor Notes

  • Non-Alarmist Tone: Security topics can feel scary. Keep it calm and empowering — "here are simple habits that keep you safe," not "the internet is dangerous."
  • Passphrases over Passwords: Emphasize that length beats complexity. A long passphrase of unrelated words is both stronger and easier to remember than "P@ss1!".
  • Private Mode Myth: Many learners believe Incognito makes them invisible. Be explicit about its limits (slide 14) — it only avoids saving data on that device.
  • Real VA Relevance: Tie PII and phishing to VA accounts and benefits — this audience is a frequent scam target. Reinforce: the VA never asks for your password by email/text.
  • Nonresponse is a Skill: Normalize NOT replying to trolls. For some veterans the instinct is to defend themselves — frame walking away as the stronger move.
  • Ergonomics for Older Learners: Spend a moment physically adjusting a chair/monitor as a demo. Small changes prevent real neck, back, and eye strain.
  • Lab Pace: The lab has 8 stations. It's fine if slower learners only finish the first few in class — the handout lets them finish at home.

📘 IC3 GS6 Level 1 Domain Coverage

  • Domain 2 — Digital Citizenship: 2.1 digital identity / PII; 2.2 reputation, permanence, legal/ethical; 2.3.1-2.3.4 negative communication, validity, anonymity, nonresponse
  • Domain 7 — Safety & Security: 7.1 threats; 7.2 passwords, reset, lock, clear browser data; 7.3 tracking, stored-data risk, private browsing; 7.4 mental & physical health risks
  • Lab → Proficiency: the "Lock It Down" lab directly exercises 7.2.3-7.2.4 (lock / clear data), 7.3.4 (private window), 2.3.2 (source validity), 2.1 (privacy self-audit), 7.3.3 (stored-data check), and 7.4.2 (ergonomics)

📦 Materials Checklist

Handouts (Print)

  • Lock It Down Lab (1 per student)
  • Security & Ergonomics Quick Reference

Technology

  • Projector connected & working
  • Each workstation powered on
  • A web browser available (Chrome/Edge)
  • Presentation open on each screen

Preparation

  • Confirm Windows + L locks the lab PCs
  • Know where "Clear browsing data" lives
  • Have a sample webpage to evaluate
  • Prepare a phishing-email example

💬 Discussion Questions

  • Opener: "What kind of personal information do you think is most important to keep private online — and why?"
  • During Validity Section: "Have you ever seen something online that turned out to be false? How did you find out?"
  • Nonresponse Section: "Is it ever better to NOT respond to someone online? When?"
  • Passwords Section: "How many different passwords do you think you have? How do you keep track of them?"
  • Wrap-up: "What's one security habit you'll start this week — a passphrase, locking your screen, or clearing your data?"

✅ Tips for Success

  • Demonstrate Windows + L live — it's instant and memorable
  • Type a sample passphrase on the projector so they see how words combine
  • Walk the room during the lab; many veterans won't ask for help out loud
  • Validate frustration with passwords and scams — it's a shared experience
  • Celebrate when someone clears their data or opens a private window for the first time
  • Keep security framing positive: simple habits, big protection

📊 No Formal Assessment This Week

Format: Week 4 has no graded test. Use the embedded 3-question knowledge check (last slides) and the hands-on lab as informal, low-stakes checks for understanding.
Looking Ahead: The Post-Test is administered next week (Week 5, Review & Testing Day). Remind students it mirrors the Week 1 Pre-Test so they can see their growth.
