Creating a Basic Document

Microsoft Word — the everyday writing tool

A word processor like Microsoft Word lets you write letters, lists, and notes — then change them anytime.

  1. Open Word
    Press the Windows key, type "Word," and press Enter. Choose "Blank document."
  2. Type your text
    The blinking cursor shows where your words will appear.
  3. Make it look good
    Use the Home tab to make text Bold, change the size, or center it.
You're already halfway there
If you can type, you can make a document. The buttons just change how it looks.

Creating a Basic Presentation

Microsoft PowerPoint — one idea per slide

A presentation is a stack of "slides." Each slide holds a short headline and a few points — like the screen you're reading now.

How to Build One

  • Open PowerPoint, choose "Blank"
  • Click in the title box, type a headline
  • Click "New Slide" to add another
  • Add pictures from the Insert tab

Keep Slides Simple

  • One main idea per slide
  • Large text — easy to read
  • A few words, not paragraphs
  • A picture is worth many words

Giving Credit to Your Sources

Referencing and attribution

Referencing means telling your reader where you got your information. Attribution means naming the person or place it came from.

🤝
Why it matters It's honest, it gives credit to the original author, and it lets your reader check the facts for themselves.
🔎
Where to find the details Look on the webpage or document for the author's name, the title, the website, and the date.
Avoid plagiarism
Copying someone's words or pictures without credit is called plagiarism. Always say where it came from.

Adding a Simple Citation

A short note that credits the source

A citation is a short line that names your source. A basic one includes the author, the title, the website, and the date you found it.

Example citation
U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs. "VA Health Care." va.gov. Accessed June 24, 2026.
💡
An easy way in Word Word has a References tab. Click "Insert Citation" to add a source and let Word format it for you.

Save & Back Up Your Work

Don't lose what you worked hard to make

💾
Save early, save often Press Ctrl + S the moment you start, and again every few minutes.
The 3-2-1 backup idea
  • 3 copies of anything important
  • 2 different places (for example, your PC and a USB drive)
  • 1 copy somewhere away from home (like the cloud)
☁️
Where to keep copies On your computer, on a USB flash drive, and in cloud storage like OneDrive or Google Drive.

Naming & Organizing Files

A good name today saves a search tomorrow

✅ Good File Names

  • VA-Benefits-Letter-2026-06.docx
  • Grocery-Budget-March.xlsx
  • Resume-John-Smith.docx

❌ Hard to Find Later

  • Document1.docx
  • untitled.docx
  • asdf.docx
Naming tips
Use words you'll remember. Add the date as YEAR-MONTH so files sort in order. Put related files in folders (like "VA Documents").

Printing: Orientation & Sides

Two choices that change how your page looks

📄 Portrait vs. Landscape

  • Portrait — tall, like a letter. The usual choice.
  • Landscape — wide, like a calendar. Good for wide tables or photos.

🔁 One-Sided vs. Two-Sided

  • Single-sided — one page per sheet.
  • Double-sided — prints on both sides. Saves paper.
Where to find these
Press Ctrl + P to open the print screen. Orientation and "Print on Both Sides" are in the list of options on the left.

Print Settings & Methods

Get exactly the pages you want

⚙️
Common settings Pick the printer, the number of copies, which pages (all or just "1-3"), and color or black-and-white.
🔍
Always check Print Preview The preview on the right shows the page before it prints — catch mistakes and save paper.
Ways to print (print methods)
A printer plugged into your PC with a cable, a wireless printer on your Wi-Fi, or Save as PDF to make a file instead of paper.

Where & How to Post Online

Choosing the right place — and following the rules

📍
Pick the right place Email for private notes, a group like Facebook for friends and family, a forum for a hobby. Match the message to the audience.
📜
Acceptable-use policies Most websites have rules about what you can post. Breaking them can get a post removed or your account closed.
Before you post, ask yourself
Is it true? Is it kind? Would I be comfortable if my family saw it? Remember — once it's posted, it can be hard to take back.

Using Inclusive Language

Words that welcome everyone

Inclusive language means choosing words that respect everyone and leave no one out.

Try This

  • "Hello everyone" / "Hi team"
  • "Folks" or "all of you"
  • Ask how someone prefers to be addressed

Instead Of

  • Assuming everyone is the same
  • Jokes that target a group
  • Words that leave people out
The simple rule
Write the way you'd want to be spoken to — with respect. When in doubt, keep it friendly and plain.

Email: Reply, Reply All & More

Four buttons that work very differently

↩️
Reply Answers only the person who sent it. Use this most of the time.
↪️
Reply All Answers the sender and everyone who got the message. Use only when all of them truly need your reply.
➡️
Forward Sends the message on to someone new who wasn't on it.
🙈
Bcc (blind carbon copy) Sends a copy to someone without the others seeing their address. Good for protecting privacy in a group email.

Effective vs. Ineffective Messages

Small habits make a big difference

✅ Effective

  • Clear subject line
  • A polite greeting and your name
  • Gets to the point
  • Re-read before sending

❌ Ineffective

  • No subject, or a vague one
  • ALL CAPS (reads like shouting)
  • Too long and rambling
  • Sent in anger
When in doubt, wait
If a message upsets you, save a draft and come back later. A calm reply is almost always a better one.

Working Together Online

Digital collaboration basics

🌟
Why collaborate? You can work on the same document from different places, see each other's changes, and finish faster — no printing or mailing.

⏱️ Synchronous

  • Happening at the same time
  • Video calls, live chat
  • Editing a doc together live

📭 Asynchronous

  • At different times
  • Email, shared comments
  • Reply when it suits you
Giving feedback
Be specific and kind. Say what works, then suggest one improvement. Use the Comments feature instead of changing someone's words directly.

Digital Etiquette

Good manners in writing and on screen

✍️ Written Etiquette

  • Be polite and patient
  • Avoid ALL CAPS and sarcasm
  • Check spelling before sending
  • Give people time to reply

📹 Visual Etiquette

  • Mute when you're not talking
  • Good lighting, tidy background
  • Look toward the camera
  • Don't talk over others
The golden rule online
There's a real person on the other side. Treat them the way you'd want to be treated.

Knowledge Check

Tap "Show answer" after you've had a think

1. You got an email sent to 10 people, but only the sender needs your answer. Which button do you use?

Reply. "Reply" goes to the sender only. "Reply All" would send your message to all 10 people — usually unnecessary and sometimes embarrassing.

2. Which file name will be easiest to find next year?

VA-Benefits-Letter-2026-06.docx. It describes what's inside and includes a date, so it's clear and sorts in order. Names like "Document1" tell you nothing later.

3. You want a tall page for a letter. Which orientation do you choose?

Portrait. Portrait is tall (like a letter). Landscape is wide (like a calendar) and is better for wide tables or photos.
