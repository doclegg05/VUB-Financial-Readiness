Make, Save & Share

Create a document, name it, cite a source, print & email it well

~35 minutes
5 parts

Today You Will

  • Create a document and save it with a clear file name
  • Add a simple citation to give credit to a source
  • Set print options the right way (no printing needed)
  • Practice email reply options on a sample message
  • Handle a reply-all and inclusive-language situation

Part 1: Create & Name a Document

~10 minGUIDED

Step 1 — Open Word

  • Open Microsoft Word and start a blank document Press the Windows key, type "Word," press Enter. Click "Blank document." (No Word? Use Word on the web at office.com, or Google Docs — the steps are the same.)

Step 2 — Type a Few Sentences

  • Write a short note — a few sentences about a benefit or service you care about Example: "Notes about VA health care. I want to learn how to refill prescriptions online and message my doctor."

Step 3 — Make It Look Good

  • Add a title and make it bold Type a title on the first line. Select it, then on the Home tab click Bold (or press Ctrl + B).

Step 4 — Save With a Good File Name

  • Save your document with a clear, descriptive name Press Ctrl + S. Choose where to save (Desktop is fine). For the name, describe what it is and add the date, like VA-Notes-2026-06.docx.
Good name rule: Describe the file + add the date as YEAR-MONTH. Use hyphens instead of spaces. Avoid names like "Document1" — they're impossible to find later.

Part 2: Add a Simple Citation

~5 minGUIDED

Step 1 — Pick a Source

  • Choose a website you used or trust For example, the VA website at va.gov. A citation tells your reader where information came from.

Step 2 — Write the Citation Line

  • At the bottom of your document, type a citation Use this pattern: Author. "Title of page." Website. Date you found it.
Example: U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs. "VA Health Care." va.gov. Accessed June 24, 2026.

Step 3 (Optional) — Let Word Do It

  • Try the References tab Click the References tab → "Insert Citation" → "Add New Source." Fill in the boxes and Word formats it for you.

Part 3: Set Print Options (No Printing!)

~5 minGUIDED

Step 1 — Open the Print Screen

  • Press Ctrl + P This opens the print screen. The big preview on the right shows your page before it prints. Do NOT click Print — we are only exploring the options.

Step 2 — Try Each Setting

  • Change the options and watch the preview Switch Orientation between Portrait (tall) and Landscape (wide). Find Print on Both Sides (double-sided). Look at Copies and Pages.

Step 3 — Save as PDF Instead of Paper

  • Choose "Microsoft Print to PDF" as the printer In the printer list, pick Microsoft Print to PDF, then click Print/Save and give it a name. This makes a PDF file instead of using paper.
SettingWhat to pick
OrientationPortrait for letters, Landscape for wide tables
SidesDouble-sided to save paper
CopiesHow many you need
Pages"All" or a range like 1-3

Part 5: Reply-All & Inclusive Language

~7 minSCENARIO

Scenario A — The Reply-All Trap

From: Maria (VUB Coordinator)
To: You + 8 other students
"Reply to let me know you got this."
  • You hit "Reply All" and typed "Got it!" — who received your message? Think it through, then write who got it and what you should have pressed instead.
The lesson: "Reply All" sent "Got it!" to all 8 other students and Maria — 9 inboxes that didn't need it. The right choice was Reply (Maria only). Always check the "To" line before you send.

Scenario B — Inclusive Language

  • Rewrite this greeting to welcome everyone in the class Original opener to a mixed group: "Hey guys, here's the homework..."
My rewrite:
One good option: "Hi everyone, here's the homework..." Inclusive language welcomes the whole group and leaves no one out.
