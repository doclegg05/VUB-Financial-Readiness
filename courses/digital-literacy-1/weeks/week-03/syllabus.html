📝 Instructor Notes

  • Reply All is the big one: Many learners have accidentally "replied all." Spend extra time here — it's the most useful and most embarrassing-to-get-wrong skill of the day.
  • Software availability: If the lab has Microsoft Word/PowerPoint, use them. If not, the free web versions (Office.com) or Google Docs/Slides work the same way — adapt the menu names.
  • No printing needed: The printing section is taught with Print Preview and "Save as PDF" so you don't waste paper or depend on a working lab printer.
  • File naming pays off: Tie naming back to the warm-up "lost file" story — a good name is how you find it again next year.
  • Citations, kept simple: Don't get into MLA vs APA. A basic "author, title, website, date" line is enough at Level 1.
  • Go slow on save vs save-as: Older learners often confuse the two. Show that the first save asks for a name and place; later saves just update.

📦 Materials Checklist

Handouts (Print)

  • Make, Save & Share Lab
  • Files, Print & Email Quick Reference

Technology

  • Projector connected & working
  • Each workstation powered on
  • Word/PowerPoint (or web versions) available
  • A USB flash drive to demo backup

Preparation

  • Test that you can open the print screen (Ctrl+P)
  • Prepare a sample email thread for Reply/Reply All
  • Confirm "Save as PDF" works on the lab PCs
  • Have a "VA Documents" folder ready to show

💬 Discussion Questions

  • Opener: "Have you ever lost a file or couldn't find one you saved? What happened — and what would have helped?"
  • During Saving/Naming: "If you opened your computer a year from now, which file name would you actually understand?"
  • Email Section: "Has anyone ever gotten a 'reply all' they wished they hadn't? When is reply-all actually the right choice?"
  • Communication: "What's one thing that makes an online message feel respectful to you?"
  • Wrap-up: "What's one document at home you'll save properly and back up this week?"

✅ Tips for Success

  • Demonstrate everything on the projector first, then let them try — show, don't just tell
  • Use real, veteran-relevant files in examples (a benefits letter, a budget)
  • Repeat keyboard shortcuts out loud: "Control... S... to save"
  • Celebrate the first person who saves a file with a good name
  • Walk the room during both lab parts — quiet learners often need help most
  • Reassure them mistakes are fine: Ctrl+Z (undo) fixes most things
