📝 Instructor Notes

  • Plain language first: Avoid jargon. Say "the box from your internet company" before introducing "modem." Define each term as you show it on screen.
  • Show, don't just tell: Networking is abstract. Whenever possible, point at the real Wi-Fi icon, the real router (if present), and a real search so it stays concrete.
  • Browser may vary: Lab PCs likely have Microsoft Edge; some students use Chrome at home. Note that tabs, bookmarks, history, and Ctrl+F work the same in all browsers.
  • Bookmarks build confidence: Helping a veteran bookmark va.gov or their email is a quick, memorable win — do it early.
  • Troubleshooting reassurance: Emphasize that restarting the router fixes most home internet problems — they don't need to be "techies" to do it.
  • Copyright, kept simple: Keep the copyright slide light — the goal is "most things online belong to someone; give credit or look for free-to-use." No legal depth needed.

🌐 Key Concepts to Cover

  • Browser parts — address bar (URL), tabs, bookmarks/favorites (star), history (Ctrl+H), back/forward/refresh
  • Connection types — wired (Ethernet), Wi-Fi, cellular; what each is best for
  • Home network gear — ISP → modem → router; the combined modem/router box
  • Connectivity check — Wi-Fi bars vs. globe icon; loading a trusted site as the real test
  • Smart search — clear keywords, relevant vs. irrelevant results, ads vs. real results, keeping sources
  • Find & reuse — Ctrl+F in pages and files; copyright, public domain, Creative Commons

📦 Materials Checklist

Handouts (Print)

  • Search & Connect Lab
  • Browser & Wi-Fi Quick Reference

Technology

  • Projector connected & working
  • Each workstation powered on
  • Internet access verified
  • A web browser open on each screen

Preparation

  • Pick a long webpage for the Ctrl+F demo
  • Decide a sample search topic in advance
  • Locate the lab Wi-Fi network name
  • Know where the lab router is (if visible)

💬 Discussion Questions

  • Opener: "When you want to look something up online, what do you do first? Do you have any websites bookmarked?"
  • During Networking: "Has your internet ever stopped working at home? What did you do? Did restarting the box help?"
  • During Searching: "Have you ever clicked a search result and landed somewhere that felt like a scam or an ad? How could you tell?"
  • Copyright: "If you found a great photo online, would you feel okay using it? When is it okay, and when should you ask first?"
  • Wrap-up: "What's one thing from today you'll try at home this week?"

✅ Tips for Success

  • Have students follow along on their own screens during the demos — doing beats watching
  • Repeat keyboard shortcuts out loud (Ctrl+F, Ctrl+H) and have them written on the handout
  • If a student's internet drops during the lab, treat it as a live troubleshooting demo for the group
  • Celebrate the first successful bookmark and the first successful Ctrl+F — these stick
  • Reassure anyone overwhelmed by networking: they only need to recognize the terms, not configure anything
  • Pair a confident student with a hesitant one during the lab if helpful
