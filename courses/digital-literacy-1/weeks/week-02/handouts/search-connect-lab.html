Part 1: Browser Navigation Drills

~6 minGUIDED

Step 1 — Open Your Web Browser

  • Open the browser on the taskbar Click the browser icon at the bottom of the screen (Microsoft Edge, Chrome, or Firefox). Or press the Windows key, type the browser name, and press Enter.

Step 2 — Use the Address Bar

  • Type a website address and go Click in the address bar at the top. Type va.gov and press Enter. The official VA website loads. Notice the .gov ending and the padlock 🔒.

Step 3 — Open a Second Tab

  • Open a new tab Press Ctrl + T (or click the small + next to your tab). A blank tab opens. Type weather.gov and press Enter. Click between the two tabs at the top.

Step 4 — Bookmark a Page

  • Save va.gov as a bookmark Go back to the va.gov tab. Click the star ⭐ at the right end of the address bar. Click Done (or Save). Now va.gov is saved for next time.

Step 5 — Use Back, Forward & History

  • Move backward and forward Click the Back ← arrow (top-left) to return to the last page. Click Forward → to go ahead again. Then press Ctrl + H to open your history list.

Checkpoint — Raise your hand when done:

  • ☐ I can open tabs, bookmark a page, and use back/forward and history

Part 2: Check Your Wi-Fi Status & Signal

~5 minGUIDED

Step 1 — Find the Network Icon

  • Look at the bottom-right of the taskbar Find the network icon near the clock. Curved bars 📶 mean Wi-Fi is connected. A globe 🌐 means you are not connected to the internet.

Step 2 — Read the Signal Strength

  • Count the bars More filled bars means a stronger Wi-Fi signal. Fewer bars can mean slower internet or dropped pages.

Step 3 — See Your Network Name

  • Click the network icon A panel opens showing nearby Wi-Fi networks. The one you're using says Connected under its name.

Part 3: Connection Troubleshooting Checklist

~5 minREFERENCE

Keep this checklist for home. If the internet stops working, try these steps in order — stop as soon as it works again.

  • 1. Check the network icon Is Wi-Fi turned on? Are you on the right network? A globe icon means you're not connected.
  • 2. Try a different website If only one site fails, the problem is that site — not your connection.
  • 3. Restart your modem/router Unplug the box, wait 30 seconds, plug it back in, and wait about two minutes for the lights to settle. This fixes most home internet problems.
  • 4. Restart your computer A simple restart clears many small glitches.
  • 5. Call your internet provider If nothing works, there may be an outage in your area. That's their job to fix, not yours.
Caution: You never need to change settings inside the router to fix a normal outage. Unplugging and plugging back in is safe and usually enough.

  • ☐ I read the troubleshooting steps and know which one fixes most problems

Part 5: Find on Page with Ctrl+F

~6 minINDEPENDENT

Step 1 — Open a Long Page

  • Visit a page with lots of text Go to va.gov or any news article with several paragraphs.

Step 2 — Open Find

  • Press Ctrl + F A small search box appears, usually in the top-right corner.

Step 3 — Search for a Word

  • Type a word you expect on the page Try benefits or health. Every match lights up. The box shows how many were found (like "3 of 8").

Step 4 — Jump Between Matches

  • Use the arrows or Enter Press Enter or click the up/down arrows to move to the next match. Press Esc to close the Find box.
