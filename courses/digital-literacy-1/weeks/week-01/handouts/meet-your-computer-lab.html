HANDS-ON LAB

Meet Your Computer

Explore the Real Computer at Your Workstation
Week 1 | VUB Digital Literacy — Level 1
~20 minutes
4 parts

Today You Will

  • Find and name the ports and devices on your computer
  • Look up how much memory (RAM) and storage your computer has
  • See which programs (apps) are installed
  • Identify which version of Windows you are using

Part 1: Find the Ports & Devices

~5 minGUIDED

Step 1 — Look at the Outside

  • Find the ports on your computer or laptop Look at the back and sides. Ports are the small openings where cables plug in. Do not unplug anything — just look.

Step 2 — Identify Each Plug

  • Find these common ports and check them off as you spot them ☐ A USB port (flat rectangle, or a small oval USB-C)   ☐ The power connector   ☐ An HDMI port (for the monitor)   ☐ A round headphone jack

Step 3 — Name Your Devices

  • Point to each device and decide: input or output? Keyboard, mouse, monitor, speakers. Remember: input goes in to the computer; output comes out to you.
One INPUT device on my desk:
One OUTPUT device on my desk:

Checkpoint — Raise your hand when done:

  • ☐ I found at least one USB port and named one input and one output device

Part 2: How Much Memory & Storage?

~6 minGUIDED

Step 1 — Open Settings

  • Open the Settings app Press the Windows key, type "settings", and press Enter. (Or click the Start button and choose the gear icon.)

Step 2 — Find Your Memory (RAM)

  • Go to System → About In Settings, click System, then scroll down and click About. Look for "Installed RAM" — this is your memory.
My computer has this much RAM (memory): GB

Step 3 — Find Your Storage

  • Open This PC to see your drive Press Windows + E to open File Explorer. Click This PC on the left. Find the bar under "Windows (C:)" — it shows free space and total size.
My (C:) drive total size is about: GB (or TB)
Tip: Memory (RAM) is the desk you work on; storage (the drive) is the filing cabinet that keeps your files. They are two different numbers!

Checkpoint — Raise your hand when done:

  • ☐ I wrote down my RAM amount
  • ☐ I wrote down my storage (C: drive) size

Part 3: Which Apps Are Installed?

~5 minGUIDED

Step 1 — Open the Start Menu

  • See your list of programs Click the Start button (bottom-left). Click "All apps" if you see it. Scroll through the alphabetical list of installed programs.

Step 2 — Spot Familiar Programs

  • Find and check off any apps you recognize ☐ A web browser (Edge or Chrome)   ☐ A word processor (Word or WordPad)   ☐ The Calculator   ☐ The Microsoft Store
Three apps installed on my computer: 1.
2.3.
Tip: These programs are application software — the apps you open to get something done. They run on top of Windows, which is the system software.

Checkpoint — Raise your hand when done:

  • ☐ I listed three apps installed on my computer

Part 4: Which Operating System?

~4 minINDEPENDENT

Step 1 — Go Back to Settings → About

  • Find your Windows version In Settings → System → About, look at the "Windows specifications" section. Find the "Edition" line — it tells you which version of Windows you have.
My operating system is Windows: (for example, Windows 11 Home)

Step 2 — Think About It

  • Connect it to today's lesson Windows is the operating system — the master program that runs the whole computer. A Mac would say "macOS," and a phone would say "Android" or "iOS."
Caution: The About page just shows information — reading it cannot change or break anything. Explore it freely. Do not change any settings unless your instructor asks you to.

Checkpoint — Raise your hand when done:

  • ☐ I identified which version of Windows my computer is running

Lab Complete!

Great work! You explored a real computer from the outside in — ports, memory, storage, apps, and the operating system.
