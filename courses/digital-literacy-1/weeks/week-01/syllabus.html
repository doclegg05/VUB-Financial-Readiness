Week 1: Inside the Computer

VUB Digital Literacy — Level 1 · IC3 GS6 Aligned

Instructor Guide
📅 Week 1 of 5 ⏱️ 2 Hours 👥 Veterans Group 📍 Computer Lab (Windows)

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

  • Identify common input and output devices 1.2.1–1.2.2
  • Recognize cables, ports, and connectors (USB, HDMI, Ethernet) 1.2.3
  • Describe desktop, laptop, tablet, and smartphone computing devices 1.4.1
  • Explain the difference between memory (RAM) and storage 1.4.2–1.4.3
  • Distinguish system software from application software 1.3.1
  • Compare proprietary and open-source software 1.3.2
  • Install software safely from a trusted source 1.3.3
  • Compare Windows, macOS, and mobile operating systems 1.5.1–1.5.2

📘 IC3 GS6 Level 1 Objective Coverage

  • 1.2 Identify digital devices and connections — input devices (1.2.1), output devices (1.2.2), cables/connectors (1.2.3)
  • 1.3 Explain fundamental software concepts — software application concepts (1.3.1), proprietary vs. open source (1.3.2), installing from online sources (1.3.3)
  • 1.4 Explain fundamental hardware concepts — computing devices (1.4.1), memory (1.4.2), data storage (1.4.3)
  • 1.5 Explain fundamental operating system concepts — mobile OS (1.5.1), computer OS (1.5.2)

Source: IC3 GS6 Level 1 Objective Domains, Certiport / Pearson VUE (released 2020-05-14).

⏰ Session Timeline (2 Hours)
0:00–0:10

Welcome & Course Kickoff Warm-Up

Introduce yourself and the 5-week Digital Literacy course. Ask: "What is one thing about your computer you've always wondered about?" Distribute handouts while students settle in.

0:10–0:30

Pre-Test Assessment Assessment

Have participants open and complete the ~20-question Pre-Test (slide 17 links to it). Reassure them it is a baseline, not a grade. Let them work at their own pace.

0:30–0:40

Objectives & Why This Matters Lecture

Review today's objectives (slides 1–2). Frame the lesson: understanding the parts of a computer makes everything in Weeks 2–5 easier and removes the fear of "breaking something."

0:40–1:00

Devices, Ports & Hardware Presentation

Slides 3–7: input/output devices, ports & connectors, device types, inside the box, memory vs. storage. Hold up a real USB and HDMI cable if available. Use the projector and point to ports on the demo PC.

1:00–1:10

Break Break

10-minute break. Encourage participants to stretch and use the restroom.

1:10–1:30

Software & Operating Systems Presentation

Slides 8–12: system vs. application software, proprietary vs. open source, installing safely, what an OS is, and Windows/macOS/mobile. Stress the "install only from trusted sources" safety point.

1:30–1:38

Knowledge Check Evaluation

Slide 13: work the three reveal questions together as a group. Let students answer aloud before you reveal each one. Clear up any confusion before the lab.

1:38–1:55

Hands-On Lab — "Meet Your Computer" Practice

Hand out the "Meet Your Computer Lab" sheet. Students find ports/peripherals, open This PC and Settings to read RAM & storage, list installed apps, and identify the Windows version. Walk the room and assist. (Exercises 1.2, 1.4, 1.5.)

1:55–2:00

Wrap-Up & Take-Home Challenge Application

Slides 14–16: recap key takeaways. Challenge: at home, find one device and name its ports. Preview Week 2 (Getting Online). Remind students to keep the quick-reference card.

📝 Instructor Notes

  • Plain language first: Lead with the everyday word ("memory," "the drive") and add the technical term second. Avoid jargon overload.
  • Use the room: The lab PCs are the best teaching aid. Point at real ports, the tower, and the Start button rather than only using slides.
  • Memory vs. storage is the key concept: Veterans often conflate the two. The "desk vs. filing cabinet" analogy lands well — repeat it.
  • Reassure on "breaking things": Many older learners fear damaging the computer. Emphasize that exploring menus and settings cannot break anything.
  • Safety without fear: When covering installs, give the rule ("only from the Store or the maker's site") without scaring them away from installing useful apps.
  • Pre-Test reassurance: Some will be anxious about a "test" on day one. Stress repeatedly that it is just a starting snapshot.

Handouts (Print)

Technology

Preparation

  • A spare USB and HDMI cable to show
  • Know where Settings → System → About is

  • Review the three knowledge-check answers

💬 Discussion Questions

  • Opener: "When you hear the word 'computer,' what comes to mind first? A desktop, a laptop, your phone?"
  • During Devices: "What devices do you plug into your computer at home? Let's name what kind of plug each one uses."
  • Memory vs. Storage: "Has anyone ever been told their computer is 'out of memory' or 'out of space'? What do you think the difference is?"
  • Software Safety: "Have you ever seen a pop-up saying your computer has a virus? What should you do with it?"
  • Wrap-up: "What's one part of the computer you understand better now than you did this morning?"

✅ Tips for Success

  • Walk around during the lab — many veterans hesitate to ask for help out loud
  • Show, don't just tell: open This PC and Settings on the projector before they try it
  • Celebrate small wins — finding their RAM number is a real accomplishment
  • Repeat the desk/filing-cabinet analogy until it sticks
  • Keep the pace gentle; it is fine to cover fewer slides well than all slides poorly
  • Validate that ports and acronyms are genuinely confusing at first — normalize the learning curve

📊 Pre-Test Notes

Purpose: Establish baseline knowledge for comparison with the Post-Test in Week 5.
Duration: 15–20 minutes (allow extra time if needed)
Format: ~20 multiple-choice questions sampling all 7 IC3 GS6 Level 1 domains
Scoring: Automatic — results saved locally for the pre/post comparison
Reassurance: Emphasize this is NOT a grade — not knowing answers is exactly why they are here.
