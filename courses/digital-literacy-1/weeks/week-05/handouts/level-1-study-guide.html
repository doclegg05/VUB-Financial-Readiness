How to use this guide: One section per topic, covering the key terms and ideas to know for the Post-Test. Read it through, then quiz yourself by covering the answers. You learned all of this in Weeks 1–4 — this just brings it together.

1 Technology Basics

Devices, hardware, software, and operating systems.

  • Input devices send info into the computer: keyboard, mouse, microphone, webcam.
  • Output devices send info out to you: monitor, printer, speakers.
  • Ports & connectors are where cables plug in: USB, HDMI, power, headphone jack.
  • Memory (RAM) is short-term workspace; it empties at shutdown. Storage (hard drive / SSD) keeps files long-term.
  • Software: system software runs the device; applications are the programs you use (Word, a browser). Install only from trusted sources.
  • Operating system (OS): the main software running a device — Windows, macOS, Android, iOS.

2 Digital Citizenship

Your identity, reputation, and behavior online.

  • PII = Personally Identifiable Information: name, SSN, birth date, address. Guard it carefully.
  • Digital privacy: control who can see your personal data; use privacy settings.
  • Permanence: anything posted can be copied or saved — it may last forever. Post as if it's permanent.
  • Reputation: act legally and ethically online; your digital footprint follows you.
  • Responding to bad content: check whether information is valid, and often the best response to negativity is no response (don't feed it).

3 Information Management

Searching and using online content responsibly.

  • Smart searching: decide what you need, use specific keywords, and judge which results are relevant.
  • Keep your sources: save the web address where you found information.
  • Ctrl + F: "Find" a word on a webpage or in a file.
  • Public domain: content free for anyone to use (no longer under copyright).
  • Creative Commons: a license that says how you may reuse a work — often free if you give credit.

4 Content Creation

Making, saving, citing, and printing your work.

  • Documents & presentations: type, format, and arrange content clearly.
  • Good file names are specific, e.g. VA-letter-2026.docx — not document1.
  • Back up your work: keep a second copy on a USB drive or in the cloud.
  • Citation: gives credit to where information came from, so it's not passed off as your own.
  • Printing: portrait = tall page; landscape = wide page. Use double-sided to save paper.

5 Communication

Expressing yourself and interacting online.

  • Where to post: follow each platform's guidelines and any acceptable-use policy.
  • Reply goes to the sender only; Reply All goes to everyone — use it carefully.
  • Forward sends a message on to someone new. Bcc hides recipients from each other (protects privacy).
  • Inclusive language: write respectfully so everyone feels welcome.
  • Effective interaction: be clear, polite, and to the point.

6 Collaboration

Working together with others online.

  • Synchronous = same time: phone call, video meeting, live chat.
  • Asynchronous = not the same time: email, shared documents — reply when convenient.
  • Benefits of digital collaboration: work together from anywhere, share files, and track changes.
  • Giving feedback: be specific and kind when reviewing a peer's work.
  • Digital etiquette: be respectful in both written and visual collaboration.

7 Safety & Security

Protecting your devices, your data, and yourself.

  • Strong passwords are long and hard to guess. A passphrase of several words works well; don't reuse passwords.
  • Lock your device when you step away: Win + L on Windows.
  • Phishing: a fake message trying to steal your info — don't click suspicious links.
  • Tracking & stored data: websites can track you; info saved on a shared PC can be seen by others — clear browser history when needed.
  • Private browsing (InPrivate / Incognito) doesn't save history on that device — useful on shared computers (but not fully anonymous).
  • Health (ergonomics): sit with good posture, take breaks, and rest your eyes.

★ Must-Know Quick Facts

The shortcuts and terms that come up most.

  • Ctrl + C copy  ·  Ctrl + V paste  ·  Ctrl + Z undo
  • Ctrl + S save  ·  Ctrl + F find  ·  Ctrl + P print
  • URL = a website's address (the "www" part you type in).
  • Wi-Fi = wireless internet connection; check the signal icon to see if you're connected.
  • Password protects an account; never share it.
