Category 1 · Technology Basics

Parts, Devices & Software

Q: A keyboard, mouse, and microphone are all examples of one kind of device. What do we call them — and what's the opposite kind?
Answer
They are input devices — they send information into the computer. The opposite is an output device (monitor, printer, speakers), which sends information out to you.
Category 1 · Technology Basics

Memory, Storage & Operating Systems

Q: What's the difference between memory (RAM) and storage, and what is an operating system?
Answer
Memory (RAM) is the short-term workspace the computer uses while it's running — it empties when you shut down. Storage (hard drive or SSD) keeps your files for the long term, even when the power is off. The operating system — like Windows, macOS, Android, or iOS — is the main software that runs the device and lets all your other apps work.
Category 2 · Digital Citizenship

Your Identity & Reputation Online

Q: What does PII stand for, and why do we say the things you post online are "permanent"?
Answer
PII means Personally Identifiable Information — details that can identify you, like your full name, Social Security number, birth date, or address. Guard it carefully. We call posts "permanent" because once something is shared online, others can copy, screenshot, or save it — so it can stay around long after you delete it. Post as if it's forever.
Category 3 · Information Management

Searching & Using Content

Q: Which keyboard shortcut finds a word on a webpage or in a document, and what does Creative Commons mean?
Answer
Ctrl + F opens "Find" to jump straight to a word on a page or in a file. Creative Commons is a type of license a creator can add to their work that tells you exactly how you're allowed to use it — often free to reuse if you give credit. (Compare with public domain content, which is free for anyone to use because it's no longer under copyright.)
Category 4 · Content Creation

Saving & Naming Your Work

Q: Why is VA-benefits-letter-2026.docx a better file name than document1.docx, and what's a simple backup habit?
Answer
A clear name tells you what's inside and when it's from without opening it — so you can find it again later. Good names are specific, with no confusing symbols. For backups, keep a second copy somewhere else — like a USB drive or a cloud folder — so one lost or broken device never costs you your work.
Category 4 · Content Creation

Citations & Printing

Q: What is a citation for, and what's the difference between portrait and landscape printing?
Answer
A citation gives credit to where you found information or an idea, so you're not taking someone else's work as your own. Portrait prints the page taller than it is wide (the normal letter layout); landscape turns it sideways so it's wider than it is tall — handy for wide tables or photos.
Category 5 · Communication

Email & Talking Online

Q: What's the difference between Reply and Reply All, and what is Bcc for?
Answer
Reply sends your message to just the original sender. Reply All sends it to everyone on the email — useful sometimes, but easy to overuse. Bcc (blind carbon copy) sends a copy to people without the other recipients seeing their addresses — good for protecting privacy when emailing a group.
Category 6 · Collaboration

Working Together Online

Q: What's the difference between synchronous and asynchronous communication?
Answer
Synchronous means at the same time — like a phone call or a video meeting where everyone is present together. Asynchronous means not at the same time — like email or a shared document, where you reply whenever it's convenient. Good digital etiquette — being clear, polite, and respectful — matters in both.
Category 7 · Safety & Security

Strong Passwords & Locking Up

Q: What makes a password strong, and which shortcut locks your Windows computer when you step away?
Answer
A strong password is long and hard to guess — a passphrase of several words (like BlueCoffeeRiver42!) works well. Don't reuse the same password everywhere. To lock your Windows PC instantly, press Win + L — always lock it when you walk away from a shared lab computer.
Category 7 · Safety & Security

Scams, Private Browsing & Your Health

Q: What is phishing, what does private browsing do, and name one way to protect your body at the computer.
Answer
Phishing is a fake message (email or text) that tries to trick you into giving up passwords or personal info — don't click suspicious links. Private browsing (an "InPrivate" or "Incognito" window) doesn't save your history or sign-ins on that device — helpful on shared computers, though it doesn't make you anonymous. For your body: take breaks, sit up with good posture, and rest your eyes (look away every 20 minutes) — that's ergonomics.
