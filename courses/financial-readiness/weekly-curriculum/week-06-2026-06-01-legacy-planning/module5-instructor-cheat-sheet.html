The 4 Things to Nail

1. The Survivor Benefit Plan (SBP) is irrevocable. 55% lifetime, Cost-of-Living-Adjusted, Defense-Department-subsidized annuity. Can't be dropped — only a rare Congressional Open Season or a new post-retirement marriage (1-yr window) reopens it. Watch for the "SBP replacement annuity" sales pitch — no private product matches it.
2. 2023 offset elimination. The Survivor Benefit Plan and Dependency & Indemnity Compensation (DIC) now stack in full — no reduction. If a student declined SBP because of the old "widow's tax" logic, that reasoning is dead.
3. Beneficiary forms override the will. An ex-spouse on a TSP-3 inherits, regardless of the will. Most repeated line of the night: "Check your beneficiaries every year."
4. Cognitive-decline 4 steps. Durable POA (verify the word "durable") • Trusted contact at each institution • Successor trustee • VA fiduciary nominee. Do it while still sharp.

Correct These Misconceptions

  • "DIC cancels out SBP." → Offset gone since 2023; they stack.
  • "My will covers everything." → Beneficiary forms win.
  • "I'll add SBP later." → Irrevocable — Open Season only.
  • "A regular POA is fine." → Must say "durable" to survive incapacity.
  • LegalZoom for a blended family. → "Forms are easy; strategy is what an attorney is paid for."

Refer Out — Don't Answer

"Should I get a trust?" • "Should I drop SBP?" • "Was declining SBP a mistake?" • any personal tax/legal/medical question → VA-accredited elder-law attorney, base legal-assistance, or a VSO (DAV/VFW/American Legion). Suspect exploitation → VA OIG 1-800-488-8244 or state Adult Protective Services.

Homework + Close

  • Assign: File VA Form 40-10007 (pre-need burial) — free, ~30 min.
  • The one sentence to repeat 3x: "Tell your family three things this month — where your documents are, who your financial contacts are, what accounts you have."
  • End well: They won't remember every number — they'll remember you gave them the tools. All materials stay on file at the VUB office.
