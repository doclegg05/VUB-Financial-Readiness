Week 4 Instructor Notes

Module 4: Managing Retirement Income • 14 slides • Current Spring 2026 packet

Use these notes with financial-readiness.html#module4, the Retirement Income Systems Map, the TSP worksheet, and the state benefits reference.

Teaching Goal

Students should leave Week 4 understanding that veteran retirement finance is not one check or one rule. It is a system: DFAS, VA, SSA, TSP/IRA custodians, IRS, Medicare, state tax agencies, and lenders all measure income differently.

The core classroom move is to separate cash flow from taxable income from benefit thresholds. That distinction is what makes the module deeper and more useful.

Suggested Flow

Time Segment Instructor Move
4:30-4:40 Warm-up Ask students to name every income source that might show up in retirement.
4:40-5:25 Slides 1-8 Teach income stacking, systems map, tax domino effect, federal taxation, and state tax.
5:25-5:35 Control calendar activity Have tables identify which decisions must happen before December 31.
5:35-5:45 Break Invite private questions, especially around personal tax situations.
5:45-6:15 Slides 9-13 + handouts Apply the systems map to debt, RMDs, inflation, and relocation decisions.
6:15-6:30 Questions and reminders Preview the no-class week and Module 5 on legacy planning.

Guardrails

  • Do not tell a student whether to do a Roth conversion, QCD, rollover, or large withdrawal. Teach the decision factors and refer them to a qualified tax professional or fee-only financial planner.
  • Use current-year language: RMDs generally begin at 73 for current retirees and 75 for those born in 1960 or later.
  • When discussing relocation, emphasize total cost of living, not just income tax.
  • VA disability compensation and CRSC are tax-free, but students should verify treatment for any means-tested program before applying.

Slide Notes

Slide 1

Title & Objectives

Explain it this way

"Today is about turning retirement income from a pile of deposits into a control panel. We are going to identify what you receive, what is taxable, what can trigger another rule, and what you can still control before year-end."

Slide 2

Income Stacking

Key points

  • Separate guaranteed income from optional withdrawals.
  • Military retired pay, Social Security, and TSP withdrawals do not have the same tax treatment.
  • VA disability compensation is tax-free and should be treated as a stabilizing cash-flow source.
Slide 3

Map Your Retirement Income

Facilitation

Give students two minutes to write down their expected income sources. Ask them to mark each as taxable, tax-free, partially taxable, or "not sure." The "not sure" column is useful because it creates the learning need for the systems map.

Slide 4

Veteran Finance Systems Map

Core distinction

DFAS, VA, SSA, and custodians are payment systems. IRS, Medicare/SSA, state tax agencies, and lenders are measurement systems. A student can have enough cash flow and still trigger higher taxable Social Security or Medicare premiums through a large traditional withdrawal.

Slide 5

The Tax Domino Effect

Teaching sequence

  • Traditional TSP/IRA withdrawal raises AGI.
  • Higher AGI can raise the taxable share of Social Security.
  • Medicare uses MAGI from two years prior for IRMAA.
  • State tax impact depends on the state and the income type.

Contrast this with VA disability compensation and CRSC, which are tax-free income sources.

Slide 6

Control the Calendar Before December 31

Activity

Use the handout's control panel. Ask each table to identify what can still be controlled before year-end: withholding, RMD completion, Roth-source versus traditional-source withdrawals, QCDs, debt payoff timing, and whether a purchase can wait.

Slide 7

Federal Taxation

Key points

  • Military retired pay and CRDP are taxable as ordinary income.
  • VA disability compensation and CRSC are tax-free.
  • Social Security taxation uses combined income: AGI + tax-exempt interest + half of Social Security benefits.
  • Large traditional withdrawals should be planned before December, not discovered at tax time.
Slide 8

State Tax Landscape

Use West Virginia as the anchor: WV exempts military retired pay, and Social Security is fully exempt from WV income tax starting in tax year 2026. Then broaden the lesson: state income tax is only one part of buying power.

Slide 9

MSG Rivera's Relocation Decision

Push students beyond "move to a no-income-tax state." Have them compare property tax, insurance, healthcare access, family support, TSP withdrawals, civilian wages, and spouse employment.

Slide 10

Debt in Retirement

Debt is not just a balance-sheet issue; it changes the monthly control panel. High-interest debt can force taxable withdrawals, which can then create federal tax, Social Security, Medicare, and state-tax consequences.

Slide 11

Managing RMDs

Emphasize responsibility and timing. The account owner is responsible for taking the correct RMD. Missing an RMD can trigger a 25% excise tax, reduced to 10% if timely corrected within two years. First RMD delay can push two RMDs into one taxable year.

Slide 12

Inflation & Buying Power

Military retired pay, VA disability compensation, and Social Security have COLA protection. Private pensions, fixed annuities, and cash savings often do not. Tie this back to household buying power, not abstract inflation statistics.

Slide 13

Key Takeaways

Close by repeating the systems language: payment source, tax form, AGI/combined income/MAGI, state rule, and debt pressure. This gives students a reusable mental checklist.

Slide 14

Module Complete

Preview the no-class week and Module 5. Encourage students to bring spouse/survivor questions to the next session because legacy planning decisions often involve family members.
